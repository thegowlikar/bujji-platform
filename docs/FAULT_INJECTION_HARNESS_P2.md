## 3. Notable real findings from building this harness

**Scenario 3's first draft was wrong, and the harness caught it.** The
initial script assumed a reconnect reporting `is_connected=True` was
itself sufficient for `RECOVERED` — it was not. The real watchdog
requires an actual fresh tick to arrive; `is_connected=True` alone
leaves it in `RECONNECTING` and it will issue further reconnect attempts
on the normal backoff schedule (confirmed: 3 reconnects fired at
`t=101, 111, 131` — exactly the real exponential schedule from Sprint
P1 — before the scripted late tick at `t=150` finally cleared it). This
is the **correct, intended** behavior — the watchdog never assumes
recovery from a weaker signal than a real tick — and the harness itself
demonstrated why that requirement matters: a naive integration (or
future refactor) that treated "socket reconnected" as "recovered" would
have been caught immediately by this exact test.

**Scenario 4's backoff timing matches Sprint P1's own design exactly**:
reconnect intervals of `10.0s` then `20.0s` — `backoff_base_seconds *
2^(attempt-1)` for attempts 1 and 2 — confirming the exponential
schedule is real, not just asserted in isolation (Sprint P1's own unit
tests already covered this; this sprint re-confirms it end-to-end
through the fake-feed integration path).

**Scenario 6 confirms a real, load-bearing design property, not just
documents it**: because the watchdog only ever activates while
`is_connected=True` (per its own real activation rule), a rapid real
disconnect/reconnect flapping pattern — exactly matching the real Sprint
P1 incident's own 502 burst timing — produces **zero** watchdog-issued
reconnects, because every real disconnect resets the state to `HEALTHY`
before 100 seconds of silence can accumulate. This means the watchdog
and the SDK's own reconnect loop do not compete or double up during a
flapping burst — confirmed behaviorally, not assumed.
